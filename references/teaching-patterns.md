# Public university teaching patterns

Research check date: 27 October 2026. The sources below are public course materials or university teaching guidance. They show practices and artifacts that instructors have made public; they do not establish a single official lecture-note standard or reveal every instructor's private preparation process.

## Observations by institution

### University of California, Berkeley

Berkeley's Center for Teaching & Learning recommends clear, student-centered, actionable, measurable learning objectives, connecting objectives to how learning will be checked and supported. Its lecture guidance advises focusing on essential ideas and difficult material, including examples and illustrations, organizing a lecture around a question or problem, and inserting discussion, pauses, and formative checks. Its active-learning page suggests short pauses for student reflection and peer discussion. These are teaching-design recommendations; they should inform notes and prompts without requiring every note to follow an identical layout.

- [Establish Course-level Learning Objectives](https://teaching.berkeley.edu/teaching-strategies/designing-your-course/establish-course-level-learning-objectives), UC Berkeley Center for Teaching & Learning, accessed 27 October 2026.
- [Lecturing Strategies](https://teaching.berkeley.edu/teaching-strategies/teaching-your-course/lecturing-strategies), UC Berkeley Center for Teaching & Learning, accessed 27 October 2026.
- [Active Learning](https://teaching.berkeley.edu/teaching-strategies/teaching-your-course/active-learning), UC Berkeley Center for Teaching & Learning, accessed 27 October 2026.

### The University of Texas at Austin

The public CE 319F course material for Spring 2020 states course and learning goals and describes assessment through tests, weekly homework, and laboratory participation. Its course resources include videos, simulations, homework, laboratory material, “terms to remember,” and interactive tools. Richard Fitzpatrick's UT Austin upper-division fluid-mechanics notes present a longer-form, hyperlinked treatment and identify their last-modified date as 27 April 2012. These illustrate both a lesson/course package and a reference-text format; neither should be mistaken for a current university-wide template.

- Kinnas, S. A. (2020, Spring). [CE 319F: Elementary Mechanics of Fluids course material](https://www.caee.utexas.edu/prof/kinnas/319lab/fmnotes/Kinnas_ce319f_Spring_2020.pdf). University of Texas at Austin.
- Fitzpatrick, R. (2012, April 27). [Fluid mechanics lecture notes](https://farside.ph.utexas.edu/teaching/336L/336l.html). University of Texas at Austin. Last modified 27 April 2012.

### Iowa State University

The AerE 344 Spring 2023 lecture 7 material on laminar and turbulent flow introduces the terms, defines Reynolds number, uses equations and plotted/illustrated material, asks students to compare cases, and lists further reading. This is a useful example of a topic-specific slide/lecture set pairing definitions and equations with visual evidence, prompts, and references. It also shows why the skill should check each course's own chosen regime thresholds and assumptions rather than copy numbers blindly.

- Hu, H. (2023, Spring). [AerE 344 lecture 7: Laminar and turbulent flows](https://www.aere.iastate.edu/~huhui/teaching/2023-01S/AerE344/class-notes/AerE344-Lecture-07-Turbulent-flow-and-BL-Control.pdf). Department of Aerospace Engineering, Iowa State University.

### Purdue University

Purdue's ME 30900 public course page for Spring 2021 organizes learning by weekly periods and topics. Topic entries link readings, lecture notes, lecture videos, and multiple worked examples; bonus material is marked as optional. Purdue's ME 200 Thermodynamics I materials similarly group tablet notes by chapter and supplement them with worked application videos and conceptual questions. The transferable pattern is consistent topic organization with separate support materials and clearly marked optional extensions.

- Wassgren, C. (2021, Spring). [ME 30900: Introduction to Fluid Mechanics course materials](https://engineering.purdue.edu/~wassgren/teaching/ME30900/). Purdue University.
- Purdue University, School of Mechanical Engineering. [ME 200: Thermodynamics I, in-class materials](https://www.purdue.edu/freeform/me200/in-class-materials/), accessed 27 October 2026.

## Reusable synthesis

For a lecturer's handout, combine these patterns into a flexible sequence:

1. State what students should be able to do and pose the main engineering question.
2. Limit the lesson to essential ideas and explain prerequisites and terminology.
3. Place labeled diagrams, plots, and physical interpretations beside the explanation they support.
4. Present equations with symbol definitions, units, assumptions, and a full worked application.
5. Add a short opportunity to retrieve, discuss, or apply the idea, then summarize the main takeaways.
6. Provide dated sources and clearly separate required content from optional extension material.

This is a practical synthesis of the sources above, not a claim that Berkeley, UT Austin, Iowa State, or Purdue requires or uses one uniform note format.
